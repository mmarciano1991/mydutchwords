# Wordkast — In-Person Usability Test
**Study 2 · Moderated, think-aloud · 1 participant · ~30 minutes**

> [!abstract] What this session is really testing
> Two gaps. First: when someone hits a Dutch word in real life, do they **instinctively reach for Wordkast** — or something else? Second: once they're in the app, **can they actually operate capture and practice** without help? The first is discoverability; the second is operability. The script tests both without giving away the answer.

> [!danger] The golden rule for the moderator
> Silence is your best tool. Never show a button, never explain a step, until the person is genuinely stuck (~40 seconds of visible struggle). Every time you rescue them, you delete the exact finding you came for.

---

## Before the session

### Setup checklist
- [ ] Phone charged; Wordkast installed and signed out (test the magic-link sign-in yourself first)
- [ ] The NOS article open in a browser tab, or the URL ready
- [ ] A real Dutch product label / printed sign in the room (for the physical-capture scenario)
- [ ] Audio recording ready; verbal consent script below
- [ ] Field sheet printed — tasks, 0–3 boxes, SEQ boxes, prompt reminders
- [ ] Your own phone silenced. Sit **beside** the participant, not across

### Recruit across levels
Across your 3 participants, include at least one person **early in their Dutch** — the app's promise is strongest for people who feel lost, and they'll hit the confusion your core user actually faces. Don't test only advanced learners.

## Session at a glance (30 min)

| Phase | Time | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0 · Consent & framing | 1 min | Permission to record; "I'm testing the app, not you" |
| 1 · Intro | 2 min | What Wordkast is for — spoken, no demo |
| 2 · Their world + baseline | 4 min | Surface their real situations; watch their natural method |
| 3 · Task A — Capture | 8 min | 3 fixed scenarios: article, label, overheard word |
| 4 · Task B — Add more, find faster way | 5 min | Discover the low-effort path (photo/share) |
| 5 · Task C — Learn (flashcards) | 5 min | Operate the practice loop end to end |
| 6 · Debrief | 5 min | Neutral, open questions — the encounter→retention gap |

*Timings are a guide. If Task A runs long, protect Task C — seeing the learning loop matters more than a third capture scenario.*

---

## 0 · Consent & framing — 1 min
*Before you start the timer or the recording.*

> "Thanks for doing this. Quick thing first: I'd like to record just the audio, so I can pay attention to you instead of scribbling. It's only for my own analysis, never shared. Is that OK?"

*(Get a clear yes before recording.)*

> "One important thing: I'm testing the app, not you. There are no wrong moves. If something's confusing or annoying, that's the most useful thing that can happen today — it means I found a problem to fix. So please don't be polite about it."

---

## 1 · Intro — what Wordkast is for — 2 min
**Spoken and warm. Do not open the app. Do not show any buttons.**

> "Wordkast is for people like you — learning Dutch while actually living here. The idea is simple: you're out in your day, you hit a Dutch word you don't know — on a sign, in a letter from the gemeente, at work, in a news article — and instead of looking it up and forgetting it thirty seconds later, you capture it in Wordkast. Then later it helps you actually learn it, so next time you see that word, you know it."
>
> "That's the whole point: catching the words your real life throws at you, so they stick."

> [!tip] Prime think-aloud (say this too)
> "As you go, please think out loud — tell me what you're looking at, what you expect to happen before you tap, and whether it matched. If you go quiet I'll just nudge you with 'what are you thinking?' — I'm not hurrying you."

---

## 2 · Their world + natural baseline — 4 min
This replaces "here's an article." You surface *their* real situations first, then watch what they naturally do — the behaviour Wordkast has to beat.

### 2a · Surface their situations (spoken, ~2 min)
> "Before you touch the app — think about your normal life here. When do you actually run into a Dutch word you don't understand? Give me a few real situations."

> [!note] Watch for
> - Let them list freely. Note each one.
> - Listen for which modality dominates: **article/news, physical label/sign, official letter, supermarket, work, spoken conversation.**
> - Their first-named situation is the most salient — you'll use it for the baseline next.

### 2b · Their natural method (baseline, ~2 min)
> "Take the one you mentioned first. Last time that happened — what did you actually do with the word? Show me, using whatever you'd normally use. Don't open Wordkast yet."

> [!note] Watch for
> - Do they copy-paste? Screenshot? Google Translate? Ask someone? Write it down? Ignore it?
> - How many steps, how much effort — this is your comparison point for everything after.
> - Note the friction in their *current* world — it's what makes Wordkast worth it (or not).

---

## 3 · Task A — Capture a word — 8 min

> [!warning] Moderator rules for all tasks
> - Sit on your hands. Count to 10 before helping. Silence surfaces the truth.
> - When they ask "how do I…?" bounce it back: *"What would you try?"* / *"Where would you expect to find that?"*
> - Rescue only after ~40s of genuine struggle — and still score it a **failure** even if they get there after your hint.
> - After each scenario: ask the SEQ (1–7) and jot the 0–3 score before moving on.

Run three fixed scenarios — the same three with every participant. They map onto the three capture methods, so a painful one points straight at a fix.

### Scenario 1 — Word in an article (digital text → paste or OCR)
*Use the two-step for this first scenario only: their way, then Wordkast.*

> "Here's a NOS article. Start reading like you normally would. When you hit a Dutch word you don't know — first, show me how you'd save it your usual way. … Good. Now do the same thing, but using Wordkast this time."

> [!note] Watch for
> - In the Wordkast step, **don't name buttons** — say "using Wordkast," not "tap the plus and paste." The hunt for *how* is the finding.
> - Do they understand the word got saved AND translated? Or unsure it worked?
> - Exact word on screen + what they tried at any "wait, how do I…" moment.

`SEQ ___ / 7 · Success 0–3 ___ · Hesitation: ____________________`

### Scenario 2 — Word on a label or sign (physical → forces photo)
*Hand them the real Dutch product/sign. No copy-paste possible — this is the OCR stress test.*

> "Here's a Dutch product label. There's a word on it you don't know. Save that word in Wordkast so you can learn it later."

> [!note] Watch for
> - Do they find the **photo/OCR path** on their own? If not, that's a top discoverability finding.
> - Does OCR actually pick the right word? Can they select just the word they want?
> - Frustration if they try to type a word they can't spell from an unfamiliar label.

`SEQ ___ / 7 · Success 0–3 ___ · Found photo path unprompted? Y / N`

### Scenario 3 — A word they heard (no text → forces manual typing)
> "Last one: imagine a colleague said a Dutch word you didn't know. You've got nothing written down — just the word in your head. Save it in Wordkast."

> [!note] Watch for
> - Do they find manual entry when photo and paste aren't options?
> - If they can't spell it, what happens? Does the app help or dead-end?
> - This is US-22 — the "heard but not seen" case. Often the most overlooked path.

`SEQ ___ / 7 · Success 0–3 ___`

> [!caution] Mercy rule (protect the session)
> If Task A is running past 8 minutes, drop Scenario 3 and cover manual entry in the debrief instead. Never sacrifice Task C (learning) for a third capture scenario.

---

## 4 · Task B — Add more, find the faster way — 5 min
Do **not** tell them to copy-paste. The point is whether they discover the low-effort path on their own.

> "Now imagine you spot a few more words worth saving — say five or six. Go ahead and add them, thinking aloud. If at any point it feels like there's a quicker or easier way than what you're doing, say so — and try it."

> [!note] Watch for
> - Do they grind the slow manual path 6 times, or look for a faster one (photo of the paragraph, share, multi-add)?
> - Fatigue/annoyance by word 3–4 — sighs, giving up early. That's your effort signal.
> - Duplicate handling — if they re-add a word, does the app tell them?

*Only if they never mention an alternative, ask at the **end** (not during):*
> "Just curious — did it feel like there was more than one way to add a word? Did you notice any other option?"

`SEQ ___ / 7 · Success 0–3 ___ · Found faster path unprompted? Y / N`

---

## 5 · Task C — Learn the words (flashcards) — 5 min
*They've committed to the app now, so directing them here is fine — discoverability matters less for practice than for capture.*

> "Now you've got some words saved. Say you've got five minutes on the train and you want to start learning them. Go ahead and do the whole practice session, start to finish. Keep thinking aloud."

> [!note] Watch for
> - Do they find how to START practice from the word list?
> - Does it feel like learning or like a quiz? Do they get why each question is asked?
> - Reaction to active recall (typing/retrieving) vs passive multiple choice — engaged or clicking through?
> - Do they reach a clear END with a sense of progress? (session summary)
> - Any word where translation, gender (de/het), or example sentence looked wrong.

`SEQ ___ / 7 · Success 0–3 ___ · Felt like progress? ____________`

---

## 6 · Debrief — 5 min
Conversational now. Open, non-leading questions. **Let silences breathe** — the best answers come after the pause.

### Broad & neutral (not "was it useful?")
1. "Walk me through your overall impression. What stood out — good or bad?"
2. "At the moment you hit that first unknown word — before you'd figured out what to do — what was going through your head?"

### The gap (your core research question)
3. "Think about how you currently deal with a Dutch word you don't know. Would this change that — honestly, or not really?"
4. "Imagine two weeks from now. Do you think you'd remember the words you saved today? What makes you say that?"

### Fit & honest adoption
5. "Who, if anyone, is this really for? Does that include you?"
6. "If you could change or add one thing, what would it be?"
7. "What felt like it was missing?"
8. "Be honest — after today, is this something you'd actually open again on your own, or probably not? … What would have to be true for you to keep using it?"

**Close:**
> "That's everything. This was really helpful — the bits where you got stuck are exactly what I needed to see. Thank you."

---

## Moderator's tips (read once before your first session)
- **The flinch is the finding.** The half-second frown or hover matters more than anything said in debrief. Hands tell the truth; words flatter.
- **Never explain unless truly stuck.** Every rescue is a data point you're deleting.
- **"That's my fault, not yours"** whenever they apologise — keeps them honest instead of self-conscious.
- **Behaviour predicts, opinions flatter.** Ask what they'd do next or when they last did this for real — not "do you like it?"
- **Write your top 3 observations within 5 minutes of each session ending,** while fresh. Don't batch to the end of all three.
