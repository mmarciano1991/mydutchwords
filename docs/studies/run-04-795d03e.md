# Run 04 — synthetic first-run walkthrough

**Build `795d03e` · 13 August 2026 · frozen**

The first run against the build that is actually deployed, and the first with
**capture from an image out of scope** — camera, gallery picker and OCR are a
deferred product decision, not a gap, so task 2 is now a typed capture task
from a real letter or sign. See the [README](./README.md).

That change is the point of this run. Task 2 has been reported as "no path"
three times, which told us nothing we didn't know after run 01 and hid what
the task is actually for: whether a person can get the words off a Dutch
letter and into their deck with a meaning they can trust. This run walks that.

Code is identical to [run 03](./run-03-43a1fdd.md)'s build (`43a1fdd`); the
merge added only the run 03 document. Tasks 1 and 3 are therefore summarised,
and task 2 is walked in full against the live behaviour.

**Environment caveat:** `en.wiktionary.org` is unreachable from this test
environment, so the online fallback could not be exercised. Words outside the
bundled dictionary land on the network-failure state instead. Everything
below distinguishes the two.

Unchanged from run 03. Account flow is clean; the two blockers are both about

## Task 2 — capture from a letter

> "Here is a letter from the gemeente. Pick out three words you don't
> recognise and add them to Woordkast. When you see the meaning the app gives
> you, tell me whether it matches what you think the word means here."

Words taken from a municipal tax letter and a street sign. Every result below
is what the app rendered.

| # | Word I typed | What the app gave me | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **aanslag** — the letter's subject line | "de aanslag — attack / assault", *and* an example: "Er was een aanslag op de ambassade. / There was an attack on the embassy." On a tax letter this means *assessment*. The example doesn't hedge it — it confirms it. I have no reason to doubt any of this, so I add it. | ❌ |
| 2 | **termijn** — "binnen zes weken" | "Did you mean **terwijl**" ("while"). Wrong, and the online lookup stays behind a link I have to read carefully. | ⚠️ |
| 3 | **weken** — same sentence | "weken — to soak", with "Week de bonen een nacht. / Soak the beans overnight." I was reading a deadline; I now have a cooking verb, presented exactly as confidently as a correct answer. | ❌ |
| 4 | **bezwaar** — how to object | "het bezwaar — objection", with "Ik heb bezwaar." Correct, useful, and indistinguishable in presentation from steps 1 and 3. | ✅ |
| 5 | **huurtoeslag** | Not in the bundled dictionary, so it goes online — which in this environment fails: *"The online dictionary didn't respond. Check your connection and try again."* plus **Try again**. The copy is honest and the retry is right; a participant on a working connection would instead get a Wiktionary gloss with no gender and no example. | ⚠️ |
| 6 | **uiterlijk** — "uiterlijk 30 september" | "Did you mean **sierlijk**" ("elegant"). Wrong. The word I wanted means *at the latest* — the single most load-bearing word on a letter with a deadline. | ❌ |
| 7 | **verkeersbord** — from the sign | Same offline failure as step 5. | ⚠️ |

**Verdict:** the task is completable — I got words into my deck without much
trouble — and that is the problem. Three of the seven were wrong, one of them
silently reinforced by a matching example sentence, and nothing on screen
distinguished them from the one that was right. A learner cannot catch this;
it's the whole reason they're using the app.

## Task 3 — practice

Unchanged from run 03 and completable in every direction: the summary offers

## New this run

| Finding | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **A wrong sense arrives with a confirming example** | This is worse than run 01 recorded. `aanslag` and `weken` don't just return the wrong meaning — they return a natural example sentence that demonstrates the wrong meaning, which is exactly the evidence a careful learner would use to check. The example makes the error more convincing, not less. |
| **Correct and incorrect results are visually identical** | `bezwaar` (right) and `aanslag` (wrong) render the same way: gender chip, gloss, example, Add to deck. There is no confidence signal, no second sense, no "other meanings" affordance, and no way to edit a translation after saving. |
| **The offline-failure copy is good** | "The online dictionary didn't respond. Check your connection and try again" plus a **Try again** button correctly separates "we couldn't reach it" from "it doesn't exist". Worth keeping as-is; noted because a participant on hotel wifi will meet it. |

## Revised task script

Task 2's framing is now permanent rather than a workaround, and its closing
question earns its place — it is the only thing in the script that surfaces a
wrong sense while the participant is still in the room. Otherwise unchanged
from run 03.

**Facilitator pre-step.** Woordkast is a web page, not an app from a store —
send the participant `<URL>` and have them open it in their phone browser.
There is nothing to install. Ask them to tap **Create an account** and
complete the email confirmation before the first task begins; if the email is
slow, the screen has a **Send it again** button. Confirm they land on a screen
saying "Add your first word".

**Task 1 — capture while reading.** *Here is a short Dutch news article, on
paper / in a second browser tab. Read it as you normally would. When you reach
a word you don't know and would want to remember, add that word to Woordkast,
then keep going. Add two or three words this way. Think aloud — especially if
the app shows you something you didn't expect.*

**Task 2 — capture from a letter.** *Here is a letter from the gemeente. Read
it the way you would if it arrived at your house. Pick three words you don't
recognise and add them to Woordkast. Each time the app shows you a meaning,
read it out and tell me whether it fits what the letter is saying.*

**Task 3 — practice what you saved.** *Practise the words you've added. Work
through them the way you would on a normal day, and stop when you feel you're
done for now. Then tell me what you'd do next.*

Bring a letter containing at least one word whose everyday sense differs from
its dictionary-first sense — `aanslag`, `termijn` and `uiterlijk` are all
reliable, and all three are ordinary words on a municipal letter. Without one,
task 2 will look like it went fine.
