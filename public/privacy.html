Privacy policy

Woordkast · last updated 17 October 2026

Woordkast is an app for learning Dutch words. This page explains what it stores about you, why, who else handles it, and how to have it deleted. In short: we keep what the app needs to work and to sync your progress, there are no ads and no tracking, and the research log is off unless you turn it on.

What is stored, and why

DataWhyLegal basis
Email address and password (stored hashed), or the account identifier Apple or Google gives us if you sign in with them Your account, so your progress follows you between devices Contract (providing the app)
Your deck: the words you saved, your edits to them, your practice results and learning progress, your daily goal, reminder time and weekly target The app itself — choosing what to practise and showing your progress Contract
Research log, only if you turn on “Help research” in Settings: each answer you give (the word, the exercise, right or wrong, how long it took, and when) The Woordkast study on how people learn vocabulary, and improving the learning method Your consent, which you can withdraw in Settings at any time
Error reports: the error message, the screen it happened on, the app version, the platform and the browser type. No account, email or deck is attached, and email addresses are removed from messages Finding and fixing crashes Legitimate interest in a working app

Without an account, everything stays on your device (in the browser’s or app’s local storage) and nothing above is sent to us except error reports. A backup file you save from Settings stays wherever you put it.

Who else handles it

  • Supabase hosts the accounts, your synced progress, the research log (if on) and error reports, in [SUPABASE REGION, e.g. the EU (Frankfurt)].
  • Hostinger serves the web app’s files. Like any web host it sees your IP address when you load the app.
  • Microsoft Azure turns Dutch words into speech for listening exercises. Only the word is sent, never anything about you.
  • Anthropic writes an example sentence for a dictionary meaning that has none. Only the dictionary meaning is sent, never anything about you.
  • Wikimedia (Wiktionary) is asked directly by your device to look up a word our dictionary doesn’t have, so Wikimedia sees that word and your IP address.
  • Apple or Google, if you choose to sign in with them, under their own privacy policies.

We don’t sell your data, show ads, or use any analytics or tracking. Typefaces are served with the app itself, not by a third party. Where a provider is outside the EU, transfers rely on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses or the EU–US Data Privacy Framework.

How long it is kept

  • Your account and deck: until you delete your account.
  • The research log: until you delete your account, or until the study ends and the data is deleted or anonymised, whichever comes first. Turning “Help research” off stops new entries and deletes the ones not yet uploaded.
  • Error reports: 90 days.

Deleting your account

In the app: Settings → Delete account. This deletes your account, your synced deck and progress, your research log and any backup copy we hold, at once, and clears the app on that device. You can also ask us by email.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct, export or delete your data, object to or restrict how it is used, and withdraw consent at any time. “Save a backup” in Settings gives you your deck as a file. You can also complain to your data protection authority (in the Netherlands, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens).

Children

Woordkast is not directed at children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect their data.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the app will say so before the change applies. The date at the top shows the latest version.
