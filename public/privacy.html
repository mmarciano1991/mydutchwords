Privacy policy

Woordkast · last updated 17 October 2026

Woordkast is an app for learning Dutch words, open to anyone, anywhere. This page explains what it stores about you, why, who else handles it, and how to have it deleted. In short: we keep what the app needs to work and to sync your progress, your data is stored in the EU, there are no ads and no tracking, and the research log is off unless you turn it on.

Woordkast is run from the Netherlands, so the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) applies to everything on this page. You get the same rights described here wherever you live.

What is stored, and why

DataWhyLegal basis (GDPR art. 6)

You don’t have to give us anything beyond an email address and password to use Woordkast, and the research log is entirely optional. We don’t make automated decisions about you that have legal or similarly significant effects; the learning schedule only decides which words to show you next.

On your device the app keeps a copy of your deck and settings in the browser’s or app’s own storage, so it works offline. A backup file you save from Settings stays wherever you put it.

Where it is stored, and who else handles it

Your account, synced progress, research log (if on) and error reports are stored by Supabase in the EU (Ireland). These service providers process data for us, under data processing agreements:

  • Supabase — database and accounts, hosted in Ireland.
  • Hostinger — serves the web app’s files. Like any web host it sees your IP address when you load the app.
  • Microsoft Azure — turns Dutch words into speech for listening exercises. Only the word is sent, never anything about you.
  • Anthropic — writes an example sentence for a dictionary meaning that has none. Only the dictionary meaning is sent, never anything about you.

Two other parties see something only when you use the feature that needs them:

  • Wikimedia (Wiktionary), in the United States, is asked directly by your device to look up a word our dictionary doesn’t have, so it sees that word and your IP address, under its own privacy policy.
  • Apple or Google, if you choose to sign in with them, confirm who you are, under their own privacy policies (Apple, Google).

Where personal data does go outside the European Economic Area (the Wiktionary lookup, Apple or Google sign-in), the transfer relies on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework or the EU Standard Contractual Clauses. We don’t sell your data, show ads, or use analytics or tracking of any kind, and the typefaces are served with the app itself rather than by a third party.

How long it is kept

  • Your account and deck: until you delete your account.
  • The research log: until you delete your account or withdraw consent, or until the study ends and the data is deleted or anonymised — whichever comes first. Turning “Help research” off stops new entries at once and deletes the ones not yet uploaded; ask us by email to delete what was already collected.
  • Error reports: 90 days.

Deleting your account

In the app: Settings → Delete account. This deletes your account, your synced deck and progress, your research log and any backup copy we hold, at once, and clears the app on that device. You can also ask us by email.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can, free of charge:

  • see the data we hold about you, and get a copy (“Save a backup” in Settings gives you your deck as a file straight away);
  • have it corrected, or deleted;
  • receive it in a machine-readable format, to take elsewhere;
  • restrict how we use it, or object to use based on our legitimate interest;
  • withdraw consent at any time, without affecting what was done before.

Email us to use any of them. You can also complain to a data protection authority: ours is the Dutch Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, and in the EU you may also go to the authority in the country where you live or work.

Children

Woordkast is meant for teenagers and adults. If you are under 16, ask a parent or guardian before creating an account, and don’t turn on “Help research” without their permission. If you believe a child has given us data without that permission, email us and we will delete it.

Security

All connections use encryption (HTTPS). Each account’s data is separated at the database level, so one user can never read another’s, and passwords are only stored as secure hashes.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the app will say so before the change applies. The date at the top shows the latest version.
